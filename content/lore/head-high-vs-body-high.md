Ask ten people what a "head high" is and you'll get ten answers — but everybody knows the feeling when they catch it. Head high, body high: it's the oldest shorthand in the game for **where** a strain hits you. Here's what's actually going on, minus the textbook energy.

## A head high lives upstairs

This is the cerebral one. Clear-eyed energy, motor-mouth conversations, that creative or giggly lift, eyes-wide focus. It's daytime flower — the stuff you reach for before you're being social or actually trying to get things done. Most people file these under **sativa**, and that's a decent starting point.

## A body high pulls you into the cushions

This is the physical one. Heavy limbs, warm and melty, the kind of deep relaxation that makes "I'll get up in a sec" a flat-out lie. Snacks hit different. It's the classic **indica** rep — what the old heads call couch-lock — and it's prime wind-down-at-night flower.

## Plot twist: it was never really indica vs sativa

Here's the part most menus won't unpack for you. Those two words started out describing how the **plant grows**, not how it makes you feel. Two jars both labeled "indica" can send you completely different places. What's actually steering your night is the strain's **chemotype** — its mix of cannabinoids (your THC, your CBD) and the aromatic compounds called **terpenes** — plus your own tolerance, your dose, and how you're built. Indica/sativa is the trailer. The terps are the movie.

## Follow your nose — the terpene cheat code

That smell when you crack the jar? That's terpenes, and it's the realest clue on the shelf for head vs body.

- **Myrcene** — earthy, musky, a little dank. It's the most common terp in cannabis and the one tied to that sinking, sedating, heavy-body feeling. Loud myrcene usually means couch.
- **Limonene** — bright and citrusy, smells like it'd wake you up. It leans uplifted and good-mood — more upstairs than down.

Real talk on the science, though: terpenes definitely run the aroma, and they **seem** to shape the experience through what people call the "entourage effect" — terps and cannabinoids playing off each other. But the research is still early, and the big synergy claims aren't locked down yet. Treat it as a strong pattern, not a promise.

## How to actually shop with this

Stop letting the THC number run your life. A 30% jar isn't "better" — it's just stronger, and stronger isn't the same as right. Instead:

- Call your shot first: you chasing a **head** night or a **body** night?
- Read the **terps and effects**, not just the potency.
- Go low and slow with anything new — you can always take more, you can't take less.
